Plenty of people assume bigger specs are always better. What really matters is **how well the specs match the workload**. Here is the practical guide.

## Know your type of workload

Workloads fall into three broad categories:

- **CPU-bound** — compiling code, encoding video, huge spreadsheets. Prioritise vCPU count.
- **RAM-bound** — nested virtual machines, large datasets, a browser with dozens of tabs. Prioritise RAM capacity.
- **GPU-bound** — 3D rendering, AI model training, cloud gaming. Prioritise VRAM and the GPU class.

## Quick reference

| Work | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Office & remote desktop | 4 vCPU · 8 GB · no GPU |
| 1080p video editing | 8 vCPU · 24 GB · 6 GB GPU |
| 3D and 4K rendering | 16 vCPU · 64 GB · 16 GB GPU |
| Fine-tuning AI models | 16 vCPU · 64 GB · 16 GB+ GPU |

## Do not forget storage

An NVMe SSD makes a large difference when opening big project files. For video editing, allow at least twice the size of your raw project so there is room for cache and rendered output.

## Start small, scale later

Our advice: take the plan one tier below your estimate, run it for a week, then watch your usage. At Xyverse an upgrade needs no re-migration — your data and configuration stay exactly as they were.
